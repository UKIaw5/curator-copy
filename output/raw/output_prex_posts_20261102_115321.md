### Executive Summary: Broken on Arrival

**"Broken on Arrival"** is a comprehensive technical audit and a novel validation tool (quantcheck) designed to detect non-functional quantized Large Language Model (LLM) artifacts in public model registries. The study addresses a critical reliability gap in the local LLM ecosystem, specifically within the **GGUF** ecosystem (utilized by **llama.cpp** and **Ollama**), where developers download pre-quantized weights without assurance of functional integrity.

### What It Is
The article presents two primary deliverables:
*   **A Large-Scale Reliability Audit:** An empirical analysis of the functional correctness of quantized models hosted in the **Ollama** official library and **Hugging Face** community repositories.
*   **quantcheck:** An open-source auditing tool that screens low-bit quantized models to identify "broken" artifacts (models that load successfully but fail to perform basic reasoning or code generation tasks) prior to deployment.

### How It Works
The methodology follows a rigorous, two-stage screening process combined with tensor-level forensic analysis:

*   **Dataset Scope:**
    *   **Ollama Official Library:** Tested all **305 eligible code-capable GGUF tags** across **15 model lines**.
    *   **Hugging Face Community:** Tested **22 popular** community-uploaded files.
    *   **Total Scope:** **327 artifacts** subjected to functional testing.
*   **Two-Stage Evaluation Protocol:**
    1.  **High-Velocity Screen:** A rapid **15-task code screen** is applied to all artifacts to quickly filter out obvious failures.
    2.  **Deep Validation:** Suspects failing the screen are retested against the full **HumanEval+** benchmark (containing **164 tasks**) to confirm non-functionality.
*   **Forensic Reconstruction & Calibration Analysis:**
    *   The authors **rebuilt five suspect files** from original high-precision weights using standard toolchains.
    *   **Tensor-Level Verification:** They performed a tensor-by-tensor comparison, confirming that the "broken" registry files were **not corrupted** (bit-identical to uncalibrated rebuilds). This proves the failure is intrinsic to the quantization process, not data corruption.
    *   **Calibration Impact:** They tested the hypothesis that calibration mitigates these failures. By holding weights, toolchain, and tensor mix constant, they added an **importance matrix** (calibration step).
        *   **Result:** This single step raised performance for **Qwen2.5-Coder-3B** builds from **0 to 76–113 solved tasks**.
        *   **Counter-Evidence:** The same uncalibrated recipe left **Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B** functional, proving that recipe alone is not a deterministic predictor of failure; context matters.

*   **Prevalence of Failure:**
    *   **7 official Ollama tags (2.3%)** were identified as non-functional, solving at most 6 of the 164 HumanEval+ tasks.
    *   **0 out of 66** sampled tags that passed the initial 15-task screen were non-functional, validating the screen's efficacy as a gatekeeper.
*   **Vulnerable Architecture Profiles:**
    *   All 7 broken artifacts were **low-bit quantizations** of models with **$\le$ 4 billion parameters**.
    *   This highlights a specific risk profile: small models are more susceptible to functional collapse during aggressive quantization.
*   **Correlation of Calibration:**
    *   The study definitively shows that **exact reproducibility (bit-for-bit matching) does not imply function**. A model can be technically "correct" in terms of file integrity yet functionally useless if the quantization parameters (like the importance matrix) are missing or misapplied.

**Executive Summary: Unlocking Any-Order Generation in Pretrained Autoregressive Image Models**

This research paper addresses a fundamental limitation in autoregressive (AR) image generation: the rigid coupling between the training objective (fixed raster-scan order) and inference capabilities. While AR models are efficient and leverage existing inference infrastructure (such as KV caching and vLLM), standard training hardcodes a single sequential factorization of the image distribution. This prevents the model from performing non-sequential tasks like inpainting, outpainting, or image editing without retraining.

The authors propose a novel adaptation recipe that transforms a standard raster-scan AR model into an "any-order" generator without the massive computational overhead associated with discrete diffusion or training from scratch.

### What This Work Is
*   **Problem Statement:** Existing solutions for flexible image generation (such as discrete-diffusion models) require training from scratch on mixed token orderings, incurring roughly **3x the compute cost** of standard raster-scan training. Furthermore, these approaches sacrifice compatibility with the optimized AR inference ecosystem (KV caching, vLLM).
*   **Core Insight:** The authors argue that the computational cost is unnecessary because a pretrained raster-scan model has already learned the joint distribution over image tokens. The model lacks not knowledge, but the *mechanism* to express that knowledge through non-sequential conditional pathways.
*   **Solution:** A low-cost adaptation recipe that unlocks latent capabilities (inpainting, outpainting, editing) from existing pretrained AR models.

### How It Works
The method employs two specific technical components to adapt the pretrained model:

*   **Future-Aware Positional Embeddings:**
    *   **Purpose:** Resolves a "target-position identifiability problem." In standard AR models, the model does not inherently know which tokens are "missing" or "targets" when generating out-of-order.
    *   **Mechanism:** These embeddings allow the model to distinguish between already-generated context tokens and target tokens to be filled, enabling the model to attend to relevant context regardless of sequence position.
    *   **Cost:** Adds negligible computational overhead.
*   **Ordering Curriculum:**
    *   **Purpose:** Creates a stable optimization path.
    *   **Mechanism:** Instead of training from scratch, the model is fine-tuned using a curriculum that gradually introduces non-sequential token orderings. This traces a path from the pretrained raster-scan solution to a robust "any-order" optimization basin.
    *   **Result:** Preserves the learned feature representations while enabling flexible generation.

### Why It Matters to Developers
*   **Significant Compute Savings:** The adaptation process costs only **13-33% additional compute** beyond pretraining, compared to the ~300% increase required by discrete diffusion baselines. This makes high-fidelity flexible generation accessible for smaller teams.
*   **Unbroken Inference Stack:** Crucially, the method leaves the standard AR inference stack untouched. Developers can continue using **KV caching** and **vLLM** optimizations, which are often incompatible with discrete diffusion architectures. This ensures low-latency inference in production environments.
*   **Superior Benchmarks:**
    *   **Inpainting:** Achieves a FID of **6.21** on ImageNet, substantially outperforming MaskGIT (FID **10.44**).
    *   **Outpainting:** Outperforms MaskGIT.
    *   **Competitiveness:** Competitive with **MAR-L** (Mask Autoregressive).
    *   **Trade-off:** Accepts a modest **1.2-1.9 increase in class-conditional FID** in exchange for the ability to perform inpainting, outpainting, and editing.
*   **Architecture Agnostic:** Validated across two architectures and two scales, proving the method is not tied to a single model family:
    *   **LlamaGen-L** and **LlamaGen-XL**
